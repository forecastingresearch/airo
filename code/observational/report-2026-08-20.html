Can LLMs Forecast Conditionals?

Told “suppose A happened” about paired ForecastBench weather questions (“will it be warmer at station X in 30 days than today?”), do models move P(B) in the right direction, by an amount that tracks the association measured in two years of resolution data? Three conditions, one chart each.

1Near-duplicates (sanity check)

Nantes and Poitiers move together almost perfectly (measured association 0.99). A working model should push P(B|A) toward 1 and P(B|¬A) toward 0.

P(B|A)P(B|¬A)

Reading: every model separates the two conditionals widely and in the right direction; none commits fully to 1. Fable 5 comes closest (0.85 vs 0.05) and the spread widens with capability.

2Positive pairs, graded

Twelve metropolitan-France pairs whose measured association ranges +0.40 to +0.93 with distance. Each dot is one pair: measured association (x) against the association implied by the model’s four elicited probabilities (y). The diagonal is perfect magnitude tracking, and the grey band is measurement uncertainty (median ±0.21 bootstrap CI on the measured association): a dot inside it is statistically indistinguishable from perfect. ρ is the rank correlation within these twelve pairs.

Reading: all four models track strength, not just existence (ρ +0.55 to +0.78), and 4–7 of 12 dots per model sit within measurement uncertainty of perfect. The consistent miss is conservatism: dots fall below the diagonal — models rank pairs nearly as well as the data allows while undershooting absolute magnitude. With twelve pairs the tier ordering is noisy here; the gradient is cleanest in the overall chart below.

3Independent pairs (controls)

Eight pairs with no shared weather driver (French stations vs. aseasonal tropical ones). The right answer is P(B|A) = P(B|¬A): a conditional shift of zero — though shifts inside the grey zone (≤0.10) are within our own measurement resolution for these pairs. Each dot is one pair’s shift; the tick is the model’s median; the dashed line is the pre-registered bar (0.075).

Reading: Haiku 4.5’s median (0.090) crosses the bar — it invents associations. The three frontier models hug zero. Fable 5’s one outlier (0.44) is a name ambiguity, not sycophancy: it read “Saint-Laurent” — French Guiana in this dataset — as one of metropolitan France’s many Saint-Laurents, and linked it to Lille accordingly.

4Overall: skill rises with capability

One summary number per model: rank correlation between implied and measured association across all 21 scored pairs (positives + controls + sanity; 0 = chance, below the cropped axis). The shaded band is the noise ceiling: an oracle that knew the true associations exactly would score ρ = 0.94 [0.89–0.98] against our finite-panel measurements, so no forecaster can be expected to beat it. Opus 5 and Fable 5 reach ~93% of the ceiling and sit inside its confidence band; Haiku 4.5 reaches 74%. X position is the model’s Epoch Capabilities Index with its 90% CI.

Design: 27 pairs of ForecastBench DBnomics station-temperature questions, future-dated 30 days; ground truth is deseasonalized association from 33 resolution sets (2024–2026), block-bootstrap certified, every scored pair with a mechanism declared before elicitation. One prompt elicits P(A), P(B), P(B|A), P(B|¬A); five repetitions; 540/540 responses. Six unscored opposite-hemisphere probe pairs excluded from all metrics.
