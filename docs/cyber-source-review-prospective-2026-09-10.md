# Cyber source review for the prospective counting instrument

Review scope: the completed main-instrument rationales from all four current models (GPT-6 Astra, Opus 5, Fable 5.1 and GPT-5.5 Pro), plus selected primary sources, checked September 10, 2026. This source check does not establish calibration or replace the final four-model numerical audit.

The lower monetary rungs have relevant empirical anchors. Those anchors do not measure the exact prospective event AIRO asks about. The new onset rules therefore resolve the definition, while the probabilities remain uncertain model judgments.

## What the retained rationales establish

Astra explicitly says it discounts pre-existing campaigns and non-material AI involvement, and distinguishes three years of incident harm from the separate catastrophe deadlines. Opus explicitly recognizes onset-window aggregation; Fable recognizes cumulative qualifying incidents and multi-year harm. Pro invokes low-threshold incident frequency and physical bottlenecks at extreme severity without an explicit numerical campaign-loss calculation. All four models separately passed the stipulated eligibility cases. A short shared rationale cannot demonstrate that every probability correctly implements those rules.

The four completed rationales describe relevant mechanisms and historical examples, but do not provide a quantitative calculation mapping campaign frequency, AI attribution, loss severity and dependence into the $22 billion or $220 billion exceedance probabilities. They provide still less quantitative support for the two extreme rungs dominating the expected-loss statistic. The original human-study comparison also differs in scope and assumptions; see the [corrected September 8 audit](cyber-validation-2026-09-10.md).

For launch, retain clear death-OR-damage labels and prospective onset dates, keep the expected-loss headline de-emphasized, and describe this as an evolving forecasting method. Keep the full responses and evidence available so the interpretation can be challenged. Do not present the source checks or comprehension diagnostic as validation of the numerical forecast levels.
